## The Story

This week's news split neatly along two fault lines: legacy brands racing to electrify their flagship nameplates, and the same brands insisting their most beloved combustion icons stay untouched. Land Rover's Range Rover Electric drew genuine praise from reviewers, while Volvo and Mercedes-Benz rolled out new electric SUVs in the 2027 Volvo EX60 and 2027 Mercedes-Benz GLC-Class EV. Meanwhile, Porsche's R&D chief made the week's most quotable promise — that the 911 will never go electric — and Lamborghini's Temerario joined the Polizia, the sixth fighting bull in that fleet since 2004.

## This Week's Top 5

**1. Range Rover Electric earns rare praise.** Reviewers walked away genuinely impressed by the Land Rover Range Rover Electric, helped by an MLA-Flex platform that was engineered for a battery pack from day one. The packaging puts the cells between the front axles, and the result reads as a luxury EV SUV that critics actually like.

**2. Porsche draws a line in the sand.** R&D chief Michael Steiner told Top Gear that the 911 will never be electric, framing faster, cheaper development cycles as the path back to health. The message: electrify the rest of the range, protect the halo car.

**3. Volvo reveals the 2027 Volvo EX60.** The new electric SUV carries the brand's familiar minimalist, Scandinavian-inspired cabin treatment. It slots into an increasingly crowded lineup of Volvo EVs.

**4. Mercedes-Benz details the 2027 Mercedes-Benz GLC-Class EV.** The GLC400 4Matic Electric Vehicle pairs front- and rear-motor all-wheel drive with five-passenger, four-door wagon packaging. It's the electric answer to Mercedes' volume seller.

**5. Lamborghini's Temerario joins the Polizia.** The Temerario becomes the sixth fighting bull in Italian police service since 2004, following the Huracan. Its duties include the "most important services," organ transport among them.

## Why It Matters

The week's coverage reveals a bifurcation in automotive strategy: legacy brands are simultaneously accelerating EV initiatives while strategically defending their combustion heritage. Range Rover, Volvo, and Mercedes-Benz are proving that electrification can be a premium proposition rather than a compromise, while Porsche's stance shows the industry still treats certain nameplates as untouchable. On the consumer side, aggressive lease deals like a Kia EV6 at $137 a month suggest inventory pressure is doing what marketing can't.

## CarMotion Daily's Take

Watch next week for whether the Range Rover Electric's warm reception translates into order books or just headlines — luxury EV enthusiasm has outpaced sales before. Porsche's 911 pledge is smart branding, but the real test is whether faster, cheaper development actually reaches showrooms. And keep an eye on EV incentives: if they keep vanishing while lease rates stay this low, the math may finally start working on its own.
