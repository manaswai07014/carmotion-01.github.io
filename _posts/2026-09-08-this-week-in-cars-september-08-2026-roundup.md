## The Story

This week's car news reveals an industry in transformation across multiple fronts. Major automakers are reshaping strategies—Honda announced a massive $9 billion cost-cutting initiative while grappling with tariff pressures—while solid-state battery breakthroughs and EV infrastructure gaps highlight the complex road ahead for electrification. Meantime, several iconic nameplates are resurging, from the Mitsubishi Pajero to the Kia Seltos positioning as the "Baby Telluride."

## This Week's Top 5

**Honda Targets $9 Billion in Cost Reductions by 2030.** The automaker behind the CR-V aims to save 1.5 trillion yen through operational efficiencies, signaling a strategic shift as the industry navigates rising costs and competitive pressures.

**Donut's Lab Achieves 409Wh/kg in Solid-State Battery Testing.** Independent verification of this energy density milestone could represent a watershed moment for EV range and performance, potentially accelerating mainstream adoption if scalable production follows.

**Mitsubishi Revives the Iconic Pajero SUV.** The boxy off-roader returns to Japan with a torquey diesel engine and selectable four-wheel drive, targeting enthusiasts who demand serious capability beyond pavement.

**EV Charging Gap Threatens Apartment Residents.** Analysis shows renters without home charging options face significant barriers to EV adoption, exposing a growing equity problem as the industry pushes toward electrification.

**2027 Kia Seltos Arrives as the "Baby Telluride."** The refreshed compact SUV reinforces Kia's three-row strategy, positioning the Seltos as an affordable alternative to its larger Telluride sibling.

## Why It Matters

The juxtaposition of Honda's cost-cutting ambitions with breakthrough battery technology tells a story of an industry managing transition costs while chasing next-generation solutions. Meanwhile, the EV infrastructure gap for apartment dwellers reveals that policy and charging access haven't kept pace with electric vehicle ambitions. The return of nameplates like the Pajero suggests automakers are hedging bets—investing in electrification while still catering to traditional off-road and utility buyers.

## CarMotion Daily's Take

Watch for further details on Honda's cost-reduction roadmap and whether other Japanese automakers follow suit. The solid-state battery claims will need close scrutiny—promising lab results don't always translate to viable production. And as tariff uncertainties persist, expect more automakers to announce strategic shifts in the coming weeks.
