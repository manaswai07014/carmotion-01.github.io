## The Story

The most powerful bike engines include the 600-hp V8 in the Boss Hoss 496, the Viper V10 in the Millyard Viper, and the 420-hp turbine engine in the MTT Y2K.

Given the lightweight nature of motorcycles, many of them can get away with using small single-cylinder engines that don't make much power — the majority of everyday bikes and scooters use this type of engine. Meanwhile, experienced riders with enough cash lying around can opt for cruisers, tourers, and superbikes with multi-cylinder engines developing triple-digit horsepower figures.

However, over the years, many enthusiasts, automakers, and boutique firms have pushed the limits of power in motorcycles to levels that defy belief. We're talking engines and power outputs that'd be considered impressive even by car standards, but are simply ludicrous in a motorcycle. Below, you'll find six of the most powerful engines ever fitted to a motorcycle — from V4s to a stonking V10 — with outputs ranging from 220 hp to over 600. We ranked them all from least to most powerful. It's quite an eclectic mix of motorcycles too, comprising production models that you can walk into a showroom and buy, track-only bikes that cost an arm and a leg, and one-off projects built by incredibly talented engineers.

Thanks to its 450-pound wet weight, the RSV4 Factory 1100 carries an exceptional power-to-weight ratio of 0.49 hp-per-pound, allowing for a top speed of over 190 mph. And what do you pay for all this searing performance?. Surprisingly, not that much, at least when compared to the other super-powerful motorcycles on this list. Aprilia has the RSV4 Factory 1100 listed at $26,749, excluding a $1,325 destination fee.

Bruno Panz/Shutterstock
Unveiled earlier this year to commemorate Ducati's 100th anniversary, the Superleggera V4 Centenario immediately wowed everyone by becoming the Italian manufacturer's most powerful street-legal motorcycle yet. It achieves this thanks to a new engine, the Desmosedici Stradale R 1100, which is based on the Panigale V4 R's 1.0-liter V4. However, Ducati has increased the piston stroke, resulting in a slightly higher 1.1-liter displacement.

In its standard configuration, the Superleggera V4 Centenario produces 228 hp at 14,500 rpm and 86.7 lb-ft at 10,500 rpm, and if you opt for the Akrapovič racing exhaust and Ducati Corse Performance oil, that output rises to 247 hp at 14,750 rpm and 93.2 lb-ft at 12,500 rpm, channeled to the road via a six-speed racing gearbox. These figures not only make the Superleggera V4 Centenario the most powerful road-going Ducati, but also the most powerful street-legal naturally-aspirated superbike in the world. If you want one, however, you're out of luck. Ducati will build only 500 examples, each priced at $165,000 before options plus a $1,495 destination fee, and all of them are already spoken for.

If these specifications tell us one thing, it's that the H2R is an absolute weapon, and Kawasaki proved this is true in the real world. In 2016, Turkish motorcycle racer Kenan Sofuoglu rocketed from a standstill to an incredible 248 mph in just 26 seconds on a stock H2R (fitted with specially-made slick tires and topped up with race-grade fuel) on Turkey's 1.67-mile-long Izmit Bay Bridge, also known as the Osman Gazi Bridge. If you find yourself itching to own a Kawasaki H2R, be prepared to part with at least $62,100, plus an $840 destination fee.

Air Marshall Motorsports/YouTube
Yes, they put an aircraft-grade turbine engine in a bike. Yes, it's street-legal. And yes, you can buy one. Made by Louisiana-based Marine Turbine Technologies (MTT), the Y2K Turbine Superbike is among the most outrageous vehicles you can own. This motorcycle comes powered by a Rolls-Royce M250-C20B turbine engine that produces 420 hp and 500 lb-ft of torque, and is mated to a two-speed automatic transmission. If you're a helicopter buff, you may recognize this turbine engine from early units of the Bell 206L LongRanger.

Thanks to its gearing-limited top speed of 273 mph, the MTT Y2K Turbine Superbike ranks as the fastest street-legal production motorcycle on the planet. However, the word "production" does do a bit of heavy lifting here, because the Y2K Turbine Superbike isn't a mass-produced motorcycle like the Aprilia RSV4 Factory 1100 or Kawasaki H2R. Instead, it's a low-volume, custom-built bike that's priced from an eye-watering $275,000, before options. If you have that sort of money, though, you'd be joining a very exclusive owners' club, which even Jay Leno is part of. And as Leno famously told Popular Mechanics when asked about his Y2K Turbine Superbike, "It's like the hand of God pushing you in the back. I've ridden a lot of fast bikes, but nothing pulls like this" (via MTT).
