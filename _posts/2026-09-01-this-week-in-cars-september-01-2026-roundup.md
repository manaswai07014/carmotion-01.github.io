## The Story

This week's auto news was dominated by trade tensions and tariffs, as the Trump administration made clear it won't budge on 50% Canadian auto tariffs set to take effect January 1. The implications ripple across the industry—from Honda's potential new U.S. factory decisions hanging on USMCA negotiations to the potential devastation of jobs and profits on both sides of the border. Meanwhile, the luxury and performance car world offered a welcome distraction, with the McLaren W1 earning rare praise and Bentley giving us our first look at its upcoming electric vehicle.

## This Week's Top 5

1. **Trump's 50% Auto Tariffs On Canada:** The administration confirmed 50% tariffs on Canadian auto exports starting January 1, with Canada vowing to retaliate. The tentative USMCA trade deal is dead, leaving the auto industry to sort out the chaos.

2. **McLaren W1 Earns Rave Review:** The W1 impressed testers at Mugello with its refreshing simplicity—just two buttons for boost and aero adjustment—earning praise as "one of the greatest cars we've ever tested."

3. **Bentley Electric Car Preview:** Despite heavy camouflage, Bentley's first EV showed a striking design that departs from the brand's traditional aesthetic, signaling a bold new direction for the British marque.

4. **GM's 6.2L V8 Recall Fix Fails:** NHTSA noticed GM's fix for the 6.2L V8 engine issue isn't actually fixing the problem, raising questions about the recall process and customer safety.

5. **Honda Factory Decision Looms:** The automaker's next U.S. factory could be built in Ohio or Mexico, with the decision hinging on the outcome of USMCA negotiations between the Trump administration and its North American partners.

## Why It Matters

The tariff situation threatens to upend decades of integrated North American automotive manufacturing. Automakers have built cross-border supply chains for efficiency, and a 50% tariff makes that math impossible. Honda's factory decision illustrates the stakes—this isn't just about trade numbers, it's about where jobs and investment will actually go. ## CarMotion Daily's Take

While the tariff rhetoric dominates headlines, it's worth noting that the luxury and performance segments continue to thrive independently. McLaren and Bentley are building cars for buyers who care about driving experience, not trade policy. But for mainstream automakers navigating these waters, the next six months will determine who survives intact. Watch for more manufacturers to announce production shifts as the January deadline approaches.
