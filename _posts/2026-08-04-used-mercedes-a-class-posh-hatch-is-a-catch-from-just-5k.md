## The Story

Mercedes' third-generation A-Class hatchback has become an affordable premium option, with well-maintained examples available for under £5,000. Launched to compete directly with the Volkswagen Golf and Audi A3, the W176 A-Class measures 4.3 metres in length and comes equipped with a range of four-cylinder petrol and diesel engines driving the front wheels. The cabin features heavily bolstered sports seats, quality plastics, and either a 7-inch or 8-inch infotainment screen depending on whether it's a pre- or post-2015 facelift model. The boot offers 341 litres of storage, while the driving experience is praised for being one of the best-handling hatchbacks of its era, with direct steering and confident cornering. The A250 variant with 208 horsepower (215hp after facelift) is highlighted as a forgotten but capable hot hatchback. Potential buyers should watch for rattles from the centre console and check the front seatbelt height adjusters on early examples.

## Why It Matters

The A-Class represents a rare opportunity to own a Mercedes with premium styling and dynamics without the typical premium price tag. Its strong residuals have softened somewhat, making it accessible to budget-conscious buyers seeking upmarket German engineering. The car's compact road feel and sporty handling distinguish it from more utilitarian rivals, while the interior quality and equipment levels remain impressive for the price point.

## CarMotion Daily's Take

The third-gen A-Class proves that Mercedes can deliver genuine driving engagement in a practical hatchback package. While the stiff ride and modest rear accommodation may not suit every buyer, those prioritising driving fun will find exceptional value in the sub-£5,000 bracket. The A200 petrol offers the best balance of economy and performance for most buyers, though the rarer A250 delivers hot hatch thrills that rival the Golf GTI. This is one used premium hatch that still feels special behind the wheel.
