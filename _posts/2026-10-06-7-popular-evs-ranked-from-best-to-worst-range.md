## The Story

The 2026 Rivian R1S with a long-range battery offers the most impressive all-electric range at 410 miles, followed by the hyper-popular Tesla Model X and Y.

For this list, we're looking at those EV best-sellers from the first half of 2026 and re-ranking them by travel distance on a single charge from best to worst. The result is a lucky seven rides that are both relatively popular and relatively free from range anxiety: the Rivian R1S, Tesla Model 3, Tesla Model Y, Cadillac Lyriq, Ford Mustang Mach-E, Chevrolet Equinox, and the Hyundai Ioniq 5. Interestingly, they seem to be split into two camps: The three popular models with the longest range were from Rivian and Tesla, while the legacy automakers were clumped very close together, all offering about 30 to 40 miles less.

Rivian
With an estimated 11,677 sales through June of 2026, the Rivian R1S was the sixth-most popular EV in the country during the first half of the year. Just as important for our purposes is that this big seven-seat SUV has the longest range on the EV top-10 bestsellers list — in some configurations, at least. Per the EPA, the R1S "Dual Max" and "Performance Dual Max" can each go up to 410 miles on a single charge of electricity on a 140-kWh battery pack. They offer the same efficiency numbers, too, since both are listed at 91 MPGe city/77 MPGe highway/84 MPGe combined equipped with 22-inch wheels.

Things get a little tricky here, though. You won't find Dual Max models of any type on the Rivian website anymore. Instead, there's a dual-motor Premium trim with the same claimed 410-mile range when ordered with a long-range battery. And again, relying on Rivian, that range doesn't change with the Power Upgrade for dual-motor models, which raises the vehicle's output from 533 horsepower to 665. For comparison, Rivian's tri-motor SUVs — the ones the brand refers to as Performance models — have a max EPA range of 370 miles. Meanwhile, the 2026 R1S quad-motor makes more than 1,000 horsepower with incredible off-road capability, but it has a range of only 338 miles.

Tesla
Tesla has been absolutely dominating the EV market this year, helped in large part by the Model 3. After notching 66,616 sales during the first six months of 2026, the brand's midsize sedan ranks second on the first-half-of-2026 EV leaderboard — almost 20,000 sales ahead of the third-place vehicle. It's also worth pointing out that — despite Consumer Reports (CR) cancelling its recommendation due to poor reliability scores in 2019 — the Model 3 is back in the organization's good graces. In fact, when we checked in on the best EVs you can buy for 2026, the Model 3 was CR's top-rated Mainstream Electric Car.

When it comes to how far it can go before recharging, well, the Model 3 is much like the R1S in one particular way: It's available in many configurations with multiple range ratings. Leading the way?. The Premium-trim model that the EPA reports can travel up to 363 miles without a recharge. That's for rear-wheel-drive editions, while a comparable all-wheel-drive Model 3 checks in with a range of 346 miles. The extra driving wheels impact the car's efficiency grades, causing the marks to slip from 145 MPGe city/128 MPGe highway/137 MPGe combined to 133/122/128, respectively.

Chanokchon/Wikimedia Commons
On the topic of Tesla domination, the Model Y had the highest sales volume of any EV in the country during the first half of this year, and that's a title it's held for over five years straight. The midsize crossover had a summer debut in 2020, becoming so hot that it became the No. 2 best-selling EV of the year despite its late start. The Model Y ascended to the sales throne in 2021 and has been there ever since. Additionally, for 2026, the Model Y's first-half sales total estimate of 163,454 marked a 9% year-over-year jump over 2025 — even though that included a 2% decrease in second-quarter sales.

The Model Y is also poised for a bit of a sales boost through the rest of 2026: The extended-length three-row Model Y L is now on sale to add to the nameplate's bottom line. Of course, that's assuming Tesla can find enough parents who want to slam their kids' heads into tempered glass. Perhaps a better selling point may be how far you can travel before recharging. The all-new 2027 Model Y L has a range of 328 miles complemented by an EPA line of 119 MPG3 city/108 MPG3 highway/114 MPGe combined. It's not that far from the regular-sized Model Y, which blends a 357-mile range with an EPA line of 144/123/134.

Cadillac
Although Cadillac is among the brands that have decommitted from an all-electric future, that was only after it had already become a small-scale EV success story. The brand sold its 100,000th all-electric model this spring. Certainly, doing its part has been the 2026 Cadillac Lyriq. With the new Lyriq-V super daily driver now in the fold, the brand's midsize EV SUV captured 7,578 sales between January and June of this year. That was enough for it to round out the first-half best-sellers list in 10th place.

The Lyriq also happens to be the longest-range EV from a legacy automaker on the list. Admittedly, the rest of the crew is close behind. According to the EPA, this luxury electric SUV can achieve a range of up to 326 miles in its rear-wheel-drive configuration — and that's with either the 11- or the 19-kW onboard charger. The three remaining choices have ranges of 318 to 320 miles.

As another advantage, the long-range Lyriq does its driving more efficiently than the Rivian R1S. The latter, remember, posted a best EPA efficiency line of 91/77/84, while the Cadillac compares at 100/83/92. Folks who prefer their Lyriqs with AWD can opt for the Performance AWD model with a 319-mile range and 89/80/89 EPA-rated mpg.
