## The Story

MotorTrend’s Jonny Lieberman drives the 2027 Mercedes-AMG GT55 and GT63, which feature up to 1,153 hp, repeatable boost, 600-kW charging, and advanced track tech.

When you activate Attack mode in the crazy-quick Porsche Taycan Turbo GT, you get 10 seconds of full power. That’s better than its Launch Control, where you only get all 1,092 horsepower for two seconds. Count ’em, two. Activate the Boost mode in the all-new, all-electric 2027 Mercedes-AMG GT55 4-Door Coupe, and the output of its three axial-flux motors increases from a healthy 557 horsepower to a full-bodied 805 hp for 55 seconds. Sure, that’s less power than the Porsche, but we should add that the GT55’s torque peaks at 1,372 lb-ft indefinitely.

No, the real crazy part is that when you reach the end of your allotted boost time, you can just pull the paddles again, and you’re back making full power. In either car. And you can keep doing it over and over again. There’s no limit, unlike in the Taycan Turbo GT where you need to wait four seconds before entering Attack mode again. Don’t feel like pulling the AMG paddles every 55/63 seconds?. Rotate the round dial on the lower righthand side of the steering wheel clockwise three times, and you’ll be in Race mode, which gives you full power until its fancy new battery is dead. I wouldn’t worry about running out of juice, though, as we did eight consecutive fast laps around a 2.7-mile-long configuration of Spain’s excellent Ascari circuit and pitted with over 60 percent of the battery left. Should you need to charge, and especially if you have the proper infrastructure, these new go-fast Mercedes-AMG GT EVs will most likely be the fastest-charging vehicles you can (soon) buy in the U.S. How?. It’s mostly about the battery.

All manner of high-tech engineering is going on underneath the rather questionable-looking skin of the electric GTs. But the big, industry-on-notice innovation is the liquid-cooled battery pack. At a roundtable discussion, Mercedes CTO Jörg Butzer wouldn’t tell us who its technical partner is, let alone the properties of the proprietary oil that cools the battery. All we know is A) it’s nonconductive, and B) it works brilliantly. Cooling battery cells is tricky. Some automakers cool them via the top or the bottom of the pack. GM, Lucid, and Rivian, for example. Others, like Tesla and BMW M, run serpentine cooling strips between the individual cells. With these new AMGs, as well as the SUV variants like the GT63 AMG SUV that will soon follow, all 2,660 cells are fully bathed in this secret cooling sauce, allowing the batteries to charge and discharge faster and longer.

We witnessed the future of AMG’s charging firsthand. While we’re fairly certain Mercedes took down the power grid in the nearby town of Ronda to keep all the press cars charged, it also brought a 1,000-kW charger to the event. Without getting into too much techie talk, the GTs are only capable of accepting 600 kW. That said, we got a taste of how well the system takes advantage of both the oil bath and its 800-volt electrical architecture, as a GT63 went from a 17 percent SOC (state of charge) to 80 percent in 10 minutes. This is roughly twice as fast as the current state of the charging art. Mercedes told us that at least one of these mega chargers will be installed at every one of its Mercedes-Benz Charging Hubs. Should you be on a road trip and in a hurry, you can reserve said big-ass charger so that you’re back on the road ASAP. No, they didn’t have a good answer for, “What if two cars reserve the charger at the same time?”.

Among the other cool tech is the diagonally linked hydraulic dampers, negating the need for anti-roll bars (as seen on some McLarens and Rivians). Mercedes calls it AMG Active Ride Control+ and it’s optional on the GT55 (replacing the air suspension) and standard on the GT63.

The cars are all-wheel drive with torque vectoring on the rear axle (both GTs feature a single motor on the front axle and two on the rear), all-wheel steering, and the front brake rotors are carbon ceramic whereas the rears are “weight-optimized steel discs.” That last bit might seem chintzy on cars starting in the mid- to high $100K range, but since the two rear motors are also involved with braking via regen, carbon-ceramic material on the rear rotors wasn’t needed.

While the liquid-cooled battery is easily the most important part of the new AMG GT EV tech, it’s not visible. Having to explain why your car is cool is never cool. To the person on the street, the showstopper is the active aerodynamics, particularly the two rear components. From front to rear, the grille shutters are active, as are the air intakes for the front brakes. Underneath both GTs (optional on the GT55, standard on the GT63), you will find two moving elements, known as Aerokinetics Venturi Flow in AMG speak. The front underbody element deploys at 75 mph, whereas the middle section opens at 87 mph. Both use the venturi effect to generate more ground-hugging downforce.

As mentioned, the real party starts at the rear of the vehicle, and for those of us of a certain age, nothing will ever be more thrilling than the first episode of season 4 of Knight Rider, when Kitt not only gets revenge against the evil Juggernaut, but is rebuilt with the perhaps a bit too Transformers-like Super Pursuit mode. The GT’s rear wing can rise to about a 45-degree angle. Much cooler is the rear diffuser that extends about 6 inches. Similar to the wing-things that slide out of the rear of the new Porsche Cayenne Turbo EV, the big moving part is weirdly cool. Does it need to exist?. Yes, and smartly, AMG included excellent software that visually shows you how the airflow around the vehicle is affected when the various moving parts are engaged. Cool and fascinating technology.
