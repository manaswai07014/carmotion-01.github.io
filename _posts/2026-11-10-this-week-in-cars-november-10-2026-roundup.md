## The Story

This week the industry split cleanly in two directions: toward nostalgia and toward power. Toyota confirmed the Celica nameplate is coming back, while Mercedes-AMG let journalists loose in the 2027 Mercedes-AMG GT55 and GT63 4-Door Coupe EVs and BMW refreshed its flagship 740 xDrive. Meanwhile, election results and regulatory fights in Washington reminded everyone that the biggest variable in the car business right now isn't engineering — it's politics. And buried beneath the headlines, the mainstream EV segment kept quietly maturing.

## This Week's Top 5

**1. The Toyota Celica returns — eventually.** Toyota has confirmed the Celica is officially making a comeback, though buyers can't have one yet. The context matters: the company posted a $26bn profit in its last financial year, a near 20 per cent drop, so enthusiasm for halo products comes with a spreadsheet attached.

**2. Mercedes-AMG's electric four-door impresses.** Our first drive of the 2027 Mercedes-AMG GT55 and GT63 4-Door Coupe EVs found the boost system genuinely entertaining — exhaust your allotted boost, pull the paddles, and full power returns.

**3. BMW updates the 2027 BMW 740 xDrive.** Four years into this generation, BMW has reworked the 7-series's front and rear design and upgraded the cabin tech. It's an evolution rather than a reinvention, but the flagship stays competitive.

**4. Emissions politics get messier.** Democrats' strong election showing could complicate the Trump administration's emissions rollback plans, even as several automakers publicly back the looser fuel economy standards. The regulatory ground is shifting under product planners' feet.

**5. Hyundai Ioniq 3 reviewed.** Hyundai offers 42kWh or 61kWh battery options, though the company says 95 per cent of Brit buyers take the larger pack despite it costing £3,500 more. It's a useful snapshot of how real buyers actually configure EVs.

Also worth noting: a 1976 VW T2 from *Matchbox: The Movie* became an actual Matchbox toy, and Renault's 547bhp rear-drive hatchback continues to prove that supercar money can buy something other than a supercar.

## Why It Matters

The week's throughline is that heritage sells, but regulation decides. Toyota can revive the Celica and Renault can build a 547bhp hatchback because enthusiasts still reward character — yet every one of those decisions is bounded by emissions rules, tariffs, and incentive structures that swing with each election cycle. Electrification itself is no longer the story; the story is who gets to sell what, where, and at what price.

## CarMotion Daily's Take

Watch two things next week: whether the Celica timeline firms up with a production date, and whether the emissions fight produces actual legislative movement rather than rhetoric. If automakers keep hedging toward hybrids and boost-heavy EVs, that tells you they're pricing in regulatory uncertainty well into the next decade.
