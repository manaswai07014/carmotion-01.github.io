## The Story

£140k for a Renault 5 is a huge ask. Here’s why the firm is confident in its unconventional supercar’s appeal and purpose.

It’s no secret that high-end electric performance cars are a tough sell at the moment, ordinarily at least. But the Renault R5 Turbo 3E is anything but ordinary. Being a 547bhp rear-drive hatch built for track driving (and drifting), it promises a heavy dose of nostalgia, supercar levels of performance, customisability and exclusivity, not to mention and a huge dose of fun to go with it, albeit for a very punchy £140k asking price. There’s almost no way to rationalise a six-figure hot hatch, but that’s potentially part of its appeal. Having ridden in the Turbo 3E around Goodwood Circuit and had its rationale explained to us by its creators, its appeal is clear as day.

Despite looking like a concept car the Turbo 3E is far from a fantasy creation, being road legal and available to reserve, with a limited production run of 1980 units. As of October 2026, half of that 1980-car run is sold out, with the process of customer specification set to begin in mid-2027. Renault names everything from the Lotus Emira to the Nissan GT-R as the Turbo 3E’s potential competitors, although there’s currently nothing like it on the market.

‘With this car, we have no real direct rival. This car is really unique,’ the car’s product lead, Florent Ingargiola, told evo. That’s because the Turbo 3E is a highly bespoke electric hot hatch that shares almost nothing with the base Renault 5. The exterior mirrors, door handles, tail lights, windscreen (albeit cut down) and the infotainment system are the only major shared components, with the Turbo 3E getting significantly widened, redesigned carbon bodywork and a unique aluminium EV platform developed by but not shared with Alpine Engineering. At 4.08 metres long and 2.03 metres wide, it’s as short as a supermini but the width of a supercar.

‘Putting the motor in the wheel, there are three benefits. First, you free up the back for aerodynamics. Second, without the transmission it cuts response time. Normally, you press the throttle, that goes to the inverters, then to the motor, then through the transmission. Here, there’s no time delay because there’s no transmission. I can assure you that the results are just incredible. You have the sensation that you have another steering wheel. Third, globally, you also save weight, partly because you can downsize all the brakes.’.

‘But the in-wheel motors are the innovation. This was the only way we could enjoy the power that we put through the motors. Otherwise we’d have to do a different weight distribution. We wanted to keep the car agile, with rear-wheel drive, with this size and weight distribution. It would have been impossible any other way.’.

Krief even admitted that the in-wheel technology has strong potential for future use by Alpine: ‘For a sporty car, the in-wheel motor is really quite promising. Today we are the first to do it, we need to industrialise it, make it efficient and reduce cost, but for us it makes a lot of sense.’.

Likewise Renault CEO François Provost, sees the R5 Turbo 3E as a proof of concept for the technology, as well as a halo supercar to elevate the brand’s status and emotional appeal: ‘If you want to differentiate yourself, you need ambition, vision and emotion’ Provost told evo.
