## The Story

Here are the best magnetic, wireless-charging, dashboard, windshield, vent, and cup-holder phone mounts for every type of vehicle and driver.

AmazonA proper phone mount should make your drive a little easier by keeping your phone secure, close by, and easy to access. It should not make your car’s interior look like a rolling electronics store. Unfortunately, there are plenty of mounts that take up half the dashboard, leave adhesive stuck to the trim, and have so many arms, clamps, and adjustment points that getting your phone in the right position becomes a project of its own.

The good news is there are plenty of better options. Magnetic mounts can keep things remarkably clean, traditional cradles offer compatibility with just about any phone, and cup-holder mounts avoid putting anything on the dashboard or windshield altogether. Some add wireless charging, while others prioritize a rock-solid grip for rough roads or off-road driving. The trick is finding one that works with your phone and your car without creating another distraction.

We researched a range of phone mounts with different mounting styles, from suction cups and air vents to cup holders and magnetic mounts. We looked at how easy they are to install and adjust, how securely they hold a phone, what kind of cases they can accommodate, and, perhaps most important’, how much they interfere with the cabin. Here are the car phone mounts we think are worth considering.

The Ram Mounts X-Grip is about as far from a minimalist phone mount as you can get, but there’s a good reason for that. The X-Grip uses four spring-loaded, rubber-coated arms to hold your phone securely without requiring a clamp around the entire device. The real advantage is Ram’s modular ball-and-socket system: You can pair the phone holder with different arms and mounting bases depending on the vehicle, giving you a huge amount of flexibility in where and how the phone sits. The holder is available in multiple sizes to accommodate different phones, with or without cases, and a safety tether is included for extra security.

Ram offers everything from suction-cup and adhesive dashboard bases to cup-holder and other mounting solutions, so it’s easy to build a setup that works without permanently modifying the interior. The trade-off is that the X-Grip system can look pretty industrial compared with a small magnetic mount. It also takes a little more effort to get the phone positioned and secured than something you can simply slap onto a magnetic pad.

Ram offers a plethora of styles and accessories, too, making its entire line of mounts super-customizable and compatible with almost any vehicle on (or off) the road, even motorcycles.

The iOttie Easy One Touch 5 is a good middle ground between a minimalist mount and a more elaborate wireless charger. The dash/windshield version uses an improved suction cup and a telescoping arm that extends from 4.5 to 6.8 inches, with 260 degrees of pivot adjustment. The cradle accommodates larger phones and cases, while iOttie’s Easy One Touch mechanism lets you squeeze the release buttons, place the phone into the cradle, and lock it in with one hand. It also rotates for portrait or landscape use.

There are several versions of the Easy One Touch 5, including dash/windshield, air-vent, CD-slot, and cup-holder mounts, so you can choose the setup that makes the most sense for your car. The air-vent version uses a twist-lock clip rather than adhesive, while the dash version uses a suction cup. A small magnetic cable organizer on the bottom of the cradle is a nice touch because it keeps the charging cable from disappearing down between the seats. It’s not the sleekest mount on this list, but it is easy to use, is highly adjustable, and doesn’t require permanently modifying the car.

The Pioneer Qi Wireless Charging Cradle is a good option if you want your phone mount to do more than just hold your phone. It uses a magnetic mount that attaches to the vehicle’s air vent and supports Qi2 wireless charging at up to 15 watts. It works with MagSafe-compatible iPhones and Android phones, while an included metal ring lets non-MagSafe phones or cases use the magnetic mount as well. The mount swivels 360 degrees, so you can position the screen in portrait or landscape orientation.

The main feature that sets the Pioneer apart is its integrated cooling fan. Wireless charging can generate quite a bit of heat, particularly when you’re using your phone for navigation at the same time, so having a small fan built into the charger helps keep temperatures under control while the phone is charging. It’s a thoughtful addition that you don’t necessarily notice until you’ve used a wireless charger that gets hot.

Setup is simple: Attach the vent mount, connect the included USB-C cable, and snap the phone into place. Silicone contact points help protect the vent, and there’s no adhesive stuck to the dash or glass. The main thing to keep in mind is that you’re still attaching something to an air vent, so compatibility depends on the design of your particular vents. For a modern car where you want to keep the dashboard clean, though, the Pioneer combines a relatively unobtrusive mount with wireless charging and active cooling in a pretty tidy package.

The iOttie Velox is designed around Apple’s MagSafe system, and its biggest selling point is how little hardware you have to deal with once it’s installed. The magnetic face holds MagSafe-compatible iPhones and cases directly, so there’s no spring-loaded cradle or side arms covering the phone. The dash/windshield version uses a suction mount and telescoping arm, with the arm extending from roughly 4–6 inches depending on the version, giving you plenty of room to get the screen where you want it. The phone can rotate between portrait and landscape, and the built-in wireless charger starts charging as soon as the phone snaps into place.
