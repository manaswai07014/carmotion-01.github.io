## The Story

Some of the most powerful pony cars ever built include the 2017-to-2024 Camaro ZL1, 2013-to-2014 Mustang Shelby GT500, and 2015-to-2023 Challenger SRT Hellcat.

When Lee Iacocca set the pony car blueprint in 1964, the recipe was straightforward: compact footprint, modest mechanicals, and an affordable price tag. Over time, though, the pony car became more outrageous. Automakers started throwing big-block V8s in legends like the 1969 Mustang GT500 and 1970 Dodge Challenger R/T Hemi, blurring the line between the pony car and its bigger cousin, the muscle car.

The Malaise Era and ever-stricter environmental laws toned down 1970s and 1980s pony cars, with 1990s models, like the 1998 Mustang SVT Cobra and the 1997 Camaro SS again reigniting the horsepower war. Still, it's the 21st century that gave us the most powerful pony cars to ever leave the factory, and it's not even close. From the 390-horsepower 2003 Ford SVT Mustang to the unhinged, 1,025-hp 2023 Challenger SRT, pony cars again dominated headlines.

Yes, we know, the Challenger's power figures, in particular, verge too much into muscle car, heck, even hypercar territory, but we'll let that pass, as it's a direct Mustang and Camaro competitor. We also love supercharged-V8 shenanigans here at Jalopnik, so every riotous version of Dodge's pony/muscle car, like the Super Stock and Demon 170, made the cut. With that settled, here are the 11 wildest pony cars to leave the factory floor!.

Chevrolet
Chevrolet let the Camaro bow down quietly after the 2024 model year, without any special, "last call" version. Not that this was strictly necessary — even in 2024, the ZL1 had more than enough oomph to satisfy seasoned Camaro enthusiasts. Packing the C7 Corvette Z06's 6.2-liter supercharged "LT4" V8 with 650 horsepower and 650 pound-feet of torque, the Camaro ZL1 catapults to 60 in 3.5 seconds. The 198-mph top speed is perhaps even more impressive, putting Chevy's pony car in the supercar league.

But the powertrain doesn't even scratch the surface of the Camaro ZL1's capability. This is a driver-oriented sports car, after all, with handling and poise atypical of pony cars. Part of the reason is the light, agile Alpha chassis, but also the suite of Nürburgring-tuned upgrades that turned Chevy's pony car into a legitimate corner carver. Most notably, the ZL1 features magnetic dampers, an electronically-controlled limited-slip differential, and Brembo calipers.

Ford
Ford did the 600-plus horsepower, overpowered pony car a few years before its Chevy rival, but with a different approach. Instead of the Camaro ZL1's advanced independent rear suspension, the S197-II-generation Mustang Shelby GT500 used an antiquated live axle, which sounds scary on a car packing 662 horsepower and 631 pound-feet of torque.

A pony car with 662 horsepower?. That's cool, but why not put a 7 in front?. That's perhaps what Dodge was thinking when Ford launched the Shelby GT500, as its pony-car monster, the Challenger SRT Hellcat, produced 707 horsepower, or up to 717 hp (after 2019) from its 6.2-liter supercharged Hemi V8. Impressive, but still on the low end for Hellcat Hemi V8s.
