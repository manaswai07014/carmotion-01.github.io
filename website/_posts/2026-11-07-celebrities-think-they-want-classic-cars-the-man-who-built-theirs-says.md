The business of high-end car restorations is extremely competitive, and business is booming. But what started out as turning 1960s and &#8217;70s Ford Broncos into status symbols for celebrities has evolved into a completely new concept of reverse restomods. Why?. Because it turns out celebrities don&#8217;t actually want an old vehicle experience, from the smell, functionality, and reliability, they just want the look.

On the latest episode of The Drivecast, Chau Nguyen, who is the founder and CEO of Vintage Modern, sat down exclusively with The Drive and explained how, and why, it&#8217;s making new vehicles look and feel old. Turns out, safety really, really matters to wealthy buyers.

While it sounds kind of elitist, I know, wealthy celebrities have a lot to lose if they are injured, or worse, in a car crash. And frankly so does Nguyen, who would be the man responsible if something bad, or worse, were to happen to someone in one of his company&#8217;s vehicles. He feels that responsibility. He says one of the big ah-ha moments for him was delivering one of his traditional restorations to LeBron James, and then panicking about what would happen if the most famous basketball player in the world got hurt in his truck.

Having experience in both traditional builds and these remasters—my word, not his—gives Nguyen unique insight into how the restoration industry has evolved over the last ten years.
