Henry Ford was always looking to economize his manufacturing process. Wood was a major ingredient in the Model T, so Ford enlisted Edward Kingsford, a cousin by marriage, to secure timberland in northern Michigan and establish a Ford-owned sawmill. But sawmills produced waste in the form of stumps, scraps, and sawdust—so why waste the waste?. Ford had Thomas Edison design a factory that produced charcoal briquettes made using a process developed by chemist Orin Stafford. By binding charcoal made from the sawmill’s wood waste with wood tar and cornstarch, Stafford’s briquettes burned with little smoke and remained intact when heated, an improvement over earlier formulations.

Ford Charcoal was originally sold through Ford dealerships, later as part of Ford-branded picnic kits that promoted car travel.
