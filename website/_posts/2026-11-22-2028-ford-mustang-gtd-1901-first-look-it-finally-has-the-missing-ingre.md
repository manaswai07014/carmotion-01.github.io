No, the Mustang GTD isn’t getting more power (yet), but Ford is finally delivering on an early promise with its new special edition. There’s a new version of Ford’s track-slaying Mustang GTD, but don’t get too excited. This is not the Mustang GTD Competition with a huge power bump that set a blistering 6:40 lap at the Nürburgring Nordschleife.

The new Mustang GTD 1901 is essentially a livery package that follows the same formula as the GTD Spirit of America special edition it replaces. You can identify the 1901 by its distinctive Royal Red paint, diagonal black graphics, and Mahogany Red leather seats with Race Red stitching.

The name and color scheme are nods to Ford corporate history. Back in 1901, Henry Ford built and raced a 26-hp, 8.8-liter two-cylinder car called Sweepstakes. Ford’s unlikely win over America’s best racer at the time, Henry Winton, allowed him to start the Ford Motor Company that still exists today. He used the recognition and credibility earned in that win to secure the financing he needed to start his second company. (He had shut down his first effort, the Detroit Automobile Company, in 1900.).

Like the Spirit of America, the 1901 comes standard with the Performance package that includes an active rear wing and front underbody flaps, a unique front fascia with dive planes, a larger front splitter and reduced sound deadening. It also debuts the GTD’s long-promised magnesium wheels, which save a significant 50.3 pounds of unsprung weight. Ford originally planned to offer the magnesium wheels when the GTD launched for 2025, but they’ve been delayed until now due to undisclosed “supplier constraints.”.

The 1901’s front splitter, diffuser, rockers, and rear wing are all finished in exposed carbon-fiber while the cabin has been dressed up with full-grain leather trim on the door panels, dashboard, and center console. The 3D-printed titanium shift paddles and gear selector dial have also been redesigned.

Mechanically, the 1901 is the same GTD that we’ve known and loved ever since we got behind the wheel. Motivation is provided by an 815-hp supercharged 5.2-liter V-8 that routes power through an eight-speed dual-clutch transaxle. A Multimatic suspension with inboard springs and dampers at the rear (and a window to watch it all work from inside the cabin!) transforms this Mustang into a monster on the race track. Ford hasn’t disclosed pricing, but expect the 1901 to start north of $400,000. Roughly 20 percent of total production (which is estimated to be between 300 and 700 cars per year) will be 1901 models and deliveries will begin in the second quarter of 2027.

Several of the 1901’s subtle tweaks will also roll out to the wider GTD line for the 2028 model year. The magnesium wheels will be available on non-1901 GTDs in black or Luster Nickel finish, and Ford is considering making them available to current GTD owners who want to upgrade their cars. There’s also a new option for off-center painted stripes and the Liquid Carbon package now features teal accents and stripes to complement its exposed-carbon-fiber body panels.

We’re still waiting for Ford to make good on its commitment to build the ’Ring-storming GTD Competition and spill the beans on exactly how much power it makes. For now, the 1901 livery and 2028 model year enhancements help keep the GTD fresh as Ford works through a backlog of customer demand.
