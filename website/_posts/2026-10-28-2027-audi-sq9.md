A racy appearance inside and out differentiates the SQ9 from its more luxury-oriented Q9 sibling; so does its price tag, which starts well north of $100,000 and goes up from there with options. All three rows of seats are relatively accommodating, although third-row legroom is at a premium with the second-row seats in their rearmost position. Under the hood of the SQ9 is a twin-turbocharged 4.0-liter V-8 engine that makes 591 horsepower. An eight-speed automatic transmission and all-wheel drive are both standard, as is an electronically controlled locking rear differential and a staggered tire fitment on either 22- or 23-inch wheels. An air-spring suspension system can be adjusted for ride height and roll stiffness while a rear-wheel-steering system can turn the rear wheels up to five degrees. When we drove the SQ9, we loved the prodigious thrust from the V-8 and found the all-wheel-drive system especially adept at putting power to the ground.

In Comparison: The BMW X7 M60i managed to hit 60 mph at our test track in 3.8 seconds, which should make for an interesting acceleration shootout once we get a chance to test the SQ9 for ourselves.

In Comparison: The SQ9 has the same 7700-pound towing maximum as the Q9. That beats the BMW's 7500-pound capacity, and falls just 16 pounds short of the Cadillac Escalade's 8100-pound max.

First Row: The SQ9 wears a similar interior design as the Q9, albeit with elements that project a racier ambiance. For example, sport seats and carbon-fiber interior trim are exclusive to the SQ9. Leather upholstery is standard and can be ordered in an array of colors, including red, black, silver, and Baikal Blue. A five-zone automatic climate control system is standard.
