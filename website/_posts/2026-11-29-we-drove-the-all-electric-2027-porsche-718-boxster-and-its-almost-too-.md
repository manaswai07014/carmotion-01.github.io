## The Story

The electric Boxster is almost here, and our prototype drive reveals surprising speed, agility, range potential, and a few fake tricks.

The electric Boxster is almost here, and our prototype drive reveals surprising speed, agility, range potential, and a few fake tricks. Seismic shifts within the automotive industry: Chinese cars went from being a literal joke to heart attack-serious bogeymen that keep American, German, and Japanese CEOs up all night. Ford makes but a single traditional car, and Toyota sells 11 different SUVs in the U.S. Alone. And now, after years of rumors, buildup, and enthusiast handwringing, Porsche will soon offer an all-electric, two-door 718 Boxster convertible. Yes, a Porsche sports car without an engine. To build hype for this impending paradigm shift (or potential sales flop), a bunch of German engineers accompanied four electric Boxster prototypes to Los Angeles to let some of us have an all-too-brief go. The results are, well, probably not what you’re thinking.

Yes, it is officially known as the 983; we still don’t know the chassis code for the 911 that replaces today’s 992, though we know it’s not 993. Back to the Boxster: Facts about the 983, like range, battery size, horsepower, torque, price, on-sale date—we have no official numbers. We couldn’t even photograph the interior. (News flash: The cockpit looks exactly like you expect from a Porsche but with white rings on the vents and door handles.) The company is playing this one close to the vest for now; expect much more information here on MotorTrend.com in late February.

We do know that the version we drove has a single motor mounted on the rear axle. There’s also a limited-slip differential, presumably an electronically controlled one. The battery sits where the engine in a combustion-engine Boxster used to reside. This is important for two reasons, according to Porsche. First, it maintains the car’s mid-engine feel, and second, the occupants are not seated on top of any batteries but are instead quite low in the vehicle, so the car retains the form factor of a two-seat, mid-engine sports car.

Even though the batteries are now stacked up as opposed to being below the floor like in, for example, the Taycan, Porsche claims the electric Boxster’s center of gravity is lower than that of both the old Boxster and the Taycan sedan. This comes down to the 983’s lower ride height. The car’s weight distribution is 40/60 front to rear, just like a 911’s. By comparison, gasoline-powered Boxsters have about 45 to 47 percent of their weight on the front wheels. While the length, height, and (we think) wheelbase remain the same, the 983 Boxster has a wider track than the 982 it replaces. We’re not sure by how much, but you can tell just by looking. Two, maybe three inches?.

Speaking of weight, Porsche told us the Boxster EV weighs as much as a 911, naturally causing us to ask, which 911?. The GT3 Leichtbau?. Or a Carrera 4 GTS Cabriolet?. The company reps wouldn’t say, but we may have caught a smile when we mentioned the (fatter) latter. For some context, according to our scales, a 992 Carrera 4 GTS weighs 3,667 pounds, a GTS Cabriolet weighs 3,799 pounds, and a hybrid Turbo S chonks in at 3,820. Figure the electric Boxster falls somewhere in that range. Getting any EV today weighing less than two tons is an accomplishment. Porsche told us the weight was a never-ending tug of war between driving range and price. Carbon-fiber bits would get the heft down but would jack up the MSRP. No, we don’t know the price, but we figure $110,000–$120,000 for the car we drove. That’s based on what the last ICE Boxster GTS stickered for when it went out of production, plus inevitable inflation, and we suspect we were behind the wheel of an electric GTS. Perhaps the base model will start closer to $80,000, going by the base 2025 Boxster’s starting ask.

The wheels are staggered and covered in Pirelli P Zero R tires. From the front we’re talking 255/35R20s; in back we’re looking at big ole 305/30R21s. If that last tire size sounds familiar, it’s because it’s the same as found on the 992.2 911 GT3, or the front of the Taycan Turbo GT Weissach with the Manthey Kit.

Speaking of the 2027 Taycans, the new Boxster features E-Shift, i.e. Fake gears and phony engine sounds. Well, maybe pre-recorded engine sounds, as Porsche mic’d up the 981 Boxster GTS’ 3.4-liter naturally aspirated flat-six because “that’s the best-sounding GTS engine.” So says Frank Moser, vice president of the 718 and 911 model lines. He ought to know. It also explains why the Boxster EV’s fake redline is 8,000 rpm, as that’s the same as the gas-burner providing the soundtrack. Fun fact: The manual version of the 911 GTS has a 7,800-rpm redline; the seven-speed PDK version is 8,000. And yes, the 983 Boxster features seven fake gears that can run in auto or manual modes via shift paddles.
