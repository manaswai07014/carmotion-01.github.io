## The Story

This week's news painted a picture of an industry hedging its bets. Legacy automakers are still chasing electrification, but they're doing it with hybrids and nostalgia rather than pure-EV conviction. Meanwhile, the performance arms kept doing what they do best: stuffing big engines into familiar shapes and charging handsomely for the privilege. The result is a market splitting into two lanes — pragmatic family haulers and increasingly exotic halo machines.

## This Week's Top 5

**GM finally commits to hybrid crossovers.** General Motors plans hybrid versions of its popular three-row SUVs, a notable strategic shift after years of pouring money into EVs. It's a tacit admission that buyers in this segment aren't ready to go fully electric.

**2027 BMW M350 xDrive tested.** The new M350 earns praise for its composed, gentler side as much as its pace — reviewers found it fast without being frantic. Early throttle in hairpins still nudges the rear out, so the playful DNA survives.

**Audi's big-SUV double act.** The 2027 Audi Q9 arrives with an MSRP range of $89,095–$103,795, while the 2027 Audi SQ9 packs a twin-turbocharged 4.0-liter V-8 making 591 horsepower. Both are comfortable and well-equipped, though the third row and haptic controls drew criticism.

**Lamborghini Revuelto gets a tribute.** The 2027 Lamborghini Revuelto lineup gains a limited-edition Revuelto Miura 60° Homage model honoring the 1960s Miura. It's heritage marketing at its most theatrical — and most effective.

**Group B legends reborn as EVs.** Renault and Peugeot revived the spirits of the Renault 5 Maxi Turbo and Peugeot 205 T16 as fully electric wide-bodied hyper hatchbacks. The comparison invites the obvious question: which one did it better?

## Why It Matters

Hybrids are no longer a stopgap — they're the compromise automakers can actually sell at volume, and GM's reversal proves it. At the same time, the top of the market keeps rewarding displacement and nostalgia, with V-8s and heritage editions commanding premium prices. That divergence suggests the next few years will be defined less by a single powertrain winner than by portfolio juggling.

## CarMotion Daily's Take

Watch whether GM's hybrid pivot spreads to its other truck and SUV lines — that would signal a broader retreat from EV-only timelines. Also keep an eye on the Renault-versus-Peugeot EV rally rivalry; if either sells well, expect every legacy badge with a motorsport past to raid its own archive. The safest bet for next week: more V-8 send-offs and more hybrid announcements.
