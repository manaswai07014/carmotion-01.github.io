Yep, it’s an extremely nerdy two-for-one Easter egg.

Root around the interior of pretty much any Vauxhall (or Opel) built in the last 20 years, and you’ll likely eventually stumble across a cartoon shark moulded into the plastic somewhere. Some of them are in obvious places, like the wireless charging pad; others, you have to go looking for in places like the glovebox hinge or the underside of the removable cupholders.

Jeep may well be the world champion of automotive Easter eggs, and they’re nowhere more prevalent than in the Wrangler, which is constantly reminding you that it’s descended from the World War II Willys Jeep. Depending on the version, you’ll find silhouettes of it in the windscreen frits, the automatic gear selector and on the wheels. Then there’s the one that flashes up on the instrument cluster on startup before it morphs into a modern Wrangler. We get it, guys.

The door speakers of the reborn electric Hummer feature what, at first glance, appear to be a standard run-of-the-mill contour map. Surely a nod to the mammoth EV’s off-road chops, right?. Not quite: what they actually depict is the Sea of Tranquility, the site of the 1969 moon landing. Despite that, we’re 99.9 per cent sure you can’t go to the moon in a Hummer EV. Although you could go to a soundstage in Nevada. Hmmm.

The long, slender bars that make up the rear lights of the new third-gen Nissan Leaf are separated out into two separate groups, one with two bars and one with three. Interpret them as Roman numerals, and it literally spells out a two and a three, at least on the right. And what’s the Japanese for ‘two-three’?. ‘Ni-san’. And yes, this is also why factory Nissan race cars have often carried the race number 23. The more you know, etc.

To celebrate their hard work, they crack open a few cold ones in the studio, until somebody realises they’ve forgotten something crucial: an exhaust tip.
